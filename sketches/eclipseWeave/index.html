Eclipse Weave

How it works

Eclipse Weave

Two flat planes, a floor and a ceiling, run away from you and meet at the horizon. There is no 3D scene: depth is worked out from one number, how far a pixel is above or below the horizon line.

7 steps. Scroll to begin ↓

Step 1 of 7

Height above the horizon

hhh is the horizon, in the middle of the screen. Moving the pointer up or down tilts the view by shifting it slightly. Pixels below it are floor, pixels above it are ceiling.

δ=y−h,h=12+0.08 (my−12)\delta=y-h,\qquad h=\tfrac12+0.08\,\big(m_y-\tfrac12\big)δ=y−h,h=21​+0.08(my​−21​)

Step 2 of 7

Depth from height

On a flat floor seen in perspective, a spot twice as far away appears half as far below the horizon. Turning that round gives depth as one over height. The 0.035 stops it blowing up at the horizon itself, and 26 caps how far you can see.

z=min⁡ ⁣(1∣δ∣+0.035, 26)z=\min\!\Big(\frac{1}{|\delta|+0.035},\ 26\Big)z=min(∣δ∣+0.0351​, 26)

Step 3 of 7

A position on the plane

Sideways position is stretched by depth, because far-away things span more ground per pixel. Adding time to the depth makes the whole plane slide toward you.

w=((x−12) z,  z+0.45 t)\mathbf{w}=\big((x-\tfrac12)\,z,\ \ z+0.45\,t\big)w=((x−21​)z,  z+0.45t)

Step 4 of 7

The weave

Two noise readings make a small offset d\mathbf{d}d that bends the plane before anything is painted on it. This warp is what makes straight bands wander like woven threads.

Step 5 of 7

Paint the two planes

The floor is a brown ramp driven by noise, lit by a grid made from two crossed sine waves. The ceiling is blue cloud noise with sine bands running through it.

grid=sin⁡(1.7 wx′+0.4 t) sin⁡(0.34 wy′−0.7 t)\text{grid}=\sin(1.7\,w'_x+0.4\,t)\,\sin(0.34\,w'_y-0.7\,t)grid=sin(1.7wx′​+0.4t)sin(0.34wy′​−0.7t)

Step 6 of 7

Haze and horizon glow

Colour fades toward a fog colour between depths 2 and 18, up to 82%. A thin bright line is added at the horizon, falling off exponentially with height.

Step 7 of 7

Smear it over time

Only a fraction β\betaβ of the fresh picture enters each frame; the rest is the previous frame, read from a few pixels away along d\mathbf{d}d turned a quarter turn. β\betaβ is 7% at its lowest and higher in the distance and near the pointer, up to 26%, so those areas look sharper. The final pass adds faint film grain and darkens the corners.
