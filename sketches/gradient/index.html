Gradient Drift

How it works

Gradient Drift

Three layers of drifting noise choose between four colours. Each frame keeps most of the last one, shifted by a pixel and a half, so the colour boundaries smear into soft trails.

5 steps. Scroll to begin ↓

Step 1 of 5

Three noise fields

Each is smooth noise between 0 and 1, drifting in its own direction at its own speed. The +100 just moves the second one far enough away to be unrelated to the first.

n1=fbm⁡(2x+0.06t)n2=fbm⁡(2x−0.045t+100)n3=fbm⁡(3x+(0.03,−0.036) t)\begin{aligned}n_1&=\operatorname{fbm}(2\mathbf{x}+0.06t)\\n_2&=\operatorname{fbm}(2\mathbf{x}-0.045t+100)\\n_3&=\operatorname{fbm}\big(3\mathbf{x}+(0.03,-0.036)\,t\big)\end{aligned}n1​n2​n3​​=fbm(2x+0.06t)=fbm(2x−0.045t+100)=fbm(3x+(0.03,−0.036)t)​

Step 3 of 5

A direction from the same noise

n1n_1n1​ is also read as an angle. Fractal noise bunches around one half, so the angle bunches around half a turn and the direction points mostly to the left.

Step 4 of 5

Keep, shift and fade

The new frame is 85% old picture and 15% fresh colour. The old picture is read from 1.5 pixels back along d\mathbf{d}d and dimmed by 5%, so colour is carried along d\mathbf{d}d. Left alone, this settles at about 78% of the fresh colour's brightness.

Step 5 of 5

Pointer glow

A soft violet glow of radius 0.15 is added around the pointer. The final pass makes the whole image breathe by 10% every 31 seconds and darkens the corners.
