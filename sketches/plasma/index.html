Plasma

How it works

Plasma

Five sine waves are added together at every pixel, and the total is turned into a colour. Nothing is remembered between frames: all the motion is the waves sliding past each other.

5 steps. Scroll to begin ↓

Step 1 of 5

Three straight waves

One runs across the screen, one up it and one diagonally, each drifting at its own speed. A factor of 10 fits about one and a half waves across the screen.

v1=sin⁡(10x+0.5t)+sin⁡(10y+0.4t)+sin⁡ ⁣(10(x+y)+0.3t)\begin{aligned}v_1&=\sin(10x+0.5t)+\sin(10y+0.4t)\\&\quad+\sin\!\big(10(x+y)+0.3t\big)\end{aligned}v1​​=sin(10x+0.5t)+sin(10y+0.4t)+sin(10(x+y)+0.3t)​

Two sets of rings

Circular waves spread outward from the centre of the screen c=(12,12)\mathbf{c}=(\tfrac12,\tfrac12)c=(21​,21​) and, at half strength, from the pointer. Subtracting ttt is what makes them travel outward.

Step 3 of 5

Squash into 0 to 1

The five waves can add up to anything between −4.5 and 4.5. Shifting and scaling brings that into the range 0.05 to 0.95.

v=v1+v2+510v=\frac{v_1+v_2+5}{10}v=10v1​+v2​+5​

Step 4 of 5

Turn the number into a colour

One more sine per colour channel, each a third of a turn behind the last. As vvv rises from 0 to 1 the colour goes once round the wheel: red, then blue, then green.

Ck=12+12sin⁡(2πv+φk),φ=(0,2π3,4π3)C_k=\tfrac12+\tfrac12\sin(2\pi v+\varphi_k),\quad\varphi=\big(0,\tfrac{2\pi}{3},\tfrac{4\pi}{3}\big)Ck​=21​+21​sin(2πv+φk​),φ=(0,32π​,34π​)

Step 5 of 5

It repeats, eventually

Every speed in the sum (0.5, 0.4, 0.3, 1 and 1.5) is a whole multiple of 0.1, so with the pointer still the whole picture repeats every 2π / 0.1 ≈ 63 seconds. The final pass lifts the mid-tones slightly and darkens the corners.
