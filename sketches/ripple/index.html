Ripple Flow

How it works

Ripple Flow

A picture that is redrawn from itself every frame. Each pixel copies a neighbour a couple of pixels away, loses 1% of its brightness, and the pointer adds light. No waves are simulated; the swirling comes from where the copying points.

4 steps. Scroll to begin ↓

Step 1 of 4

A wind made of noise

Each pixel gets two noise values, one per axis, rescaled to run from −1 to 1. Together they act as a wind direction that changes smoothly across the screen and drifts over time.

f=2(fbm⁡(3x+0.1t)fbm⁡(3x+0.1t+10))−1\mathbf{f}=2\begin{pmatrix}\operatorname{fbm}(3\mathbf{x}+0.1t)\\[2pt]\operatorname{fbm}(3\mathbf{x}+0.1t+10)\end{pmatrix}-1f=2(fbm(3x+0.1t)fbm(3x+0.1t+10)​)−1

Step 2 of 4

Copy from upwind

The new frame reads the old one from up to two pixels against that wind and keeps 99% of it. Repeated every frame, bright areas slide along the wind and stretch into streaks.

S′(x)=0.99  S ⁣(x−2 fR)S'(\mathbf{x})=0.99\;S\!\Big(\mathbf{x}-\frac{2\,\mathbf{f}}{\mathbf{R}}\Big)S′(x)=0.99S(x−R2f​)

Step 3 of 4

The pointer adds light

A soft spot around the pointer is added on top. It adds up to 2 per frame while only 1% fades, so the spot goes white at once. The radius is 0.05 of the screen in each direction, which makes it an oval on a wide screen.

S′+=2 (1−smoothstep⁡(0, 0.05, ∥x−m∥))S'\mathrel{+}=2\,\big(1-\operatorname{smoothstep}(0,\,0.05,\,\lVert\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{m}\rVert)\big)S′+=2(1−smoothstep(0,0.05,∥x−m∥))

Step 4 of 4

How long light lasts

Losing 1% per frame halves the brightness every 69 frames: a little over a second at 60 frames per second, half that on a 120 Hz display. The final pass only darkens the corners.

0.9969≈120.99^{69}\approx\tfrac120.9969≈21​
