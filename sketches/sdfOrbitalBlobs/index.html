How it works

SDF Orbital Blobs

Seven spheres are described by a single function that gives the distance from any point in space to the nearest surface. Rays are walked through that function to find what each pixel sees, and the spheres are merged so that they melt into one another.

Step 1 of 7

Six moving spheres and one still one

Sphere iii circles the origin at its own speed, bobbing up and down as it goes, while its radius pulses. The 1.047 is a sixth of a turn, which spaces the six evenly at the start. A seventh sphere of radius 0.42 sits at the centre.

Step 3 of 7

Melt them together

Taking the smallest of the seven distances would give seven separate balls. blend⁡\operatorname{blend}blend rounds off the crease wherever two surfaces come close, so they join with a smooth neck. The six orbiting spheres are folded together with a width of 0.44, then the centre sphere is added with 0.53.

Step 4 of 7

Walk a ray

For each pixel a ray leaves the camera in direction r^\hat{\mathbf{r}}r^. DDD says how far away the nearest surface is, so it is always safe to move that far. The shader moves 85% of it and repeats, up to 90 times, until DDD drops under 0.001 (a hit) or the ray has gone 12 units (a miss). The pointer moves the camera sideways.

Step 6 of 7

Light it

l\mathbf{l}l points at the light and v\mathbf{v}v back at the camera. Diffuse light depends on how squarely the surface faces the light. The highlight appears where the light's mirror reflection l′\mathbf{l}'l′ lines up with the camera, and the 24th power keeps it small. The rim term brightens edges seen at a glancing angle. The final colour is the sphere's own colour times 0.18+0.8×0.18+0.8\times0.18+0.8× diffuse, plus the highlight and a pale blue rim.

Step 7 of 7

Afterglow

Only 20% of each new frame is mixed in. The other 80% is the previous frame, slightly dimmed and shifted by k\mathbf{k}k, 0.8 pixels toward the lower right. That leaves a soft halo trailing behind the moving blobs.
