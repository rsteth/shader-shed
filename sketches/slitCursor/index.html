How it works

Slit Bands · Cursor Brush

A brush that follows the pointer. It stretches the faster it moves and leaves a trail that cools and fades. With no pointer on the canvas, a slow figure-eight stands in.

8 steps. Scroll to begin ↓

Step 1 of 8

Stretch with speed

u\mathbf{u}u is the pointer's velocity and u^\hat{\mathbf{u}}u^ its direction. The brush is a circle whose coordinate along the direction of travel is divided by kkk, which makes it up to 2.6 times longer that way.

Step 2 of 8

The stand-in

q\mathbf{q}q is the pixel's position relative to the brush centre. With no pointer, the centre follows this path instead. The vertical part runs at twice the rate of the horizontal part, which draws a figure-eight.

Step 3 of 8

Store the shape, not a picture

The first pass draws nothing you see. For every pixel it stores how deep inside the outline that pixel is, as a number from 0 to 1 where 12\tfrac1221​ is exactly on the edge.

ink=clamp⁡ ⁣(12−d0.12, 0, 1)\text{ink}=\operatorname{clamp}\!\Big(\tfrac12-\frac{d}{0.12},\ 0,\ 1\Big)ink=clamp(21​−0.12d​, 0, 1)

Step 4 of 8

A fading trail

Where the shape was a moment ago, the stored ink is kept and counts down from 1 to the 12\tfrac1221​ cut-off over 1.6 seconds. Those bars dim and slide toward the cool end of the spectrum, then switch off.

ink′=max⁡ ⁣(ink, inkprev−Δt3.2)\text{ink}'=\max\!\Big(\text{ink},\ \text{ink}_{\text{prev}}-\frac{\Delta t}{3.2}\Big)ink′=max(ink, inkprev​−3.2Δt​)

Step 5 of 8

Colour the left and right edges

nxn_xnx​ says which way the outline faces at that spot: −1-1−1 for left, +1+1+1 for right. eee is 1 on the edge and fades to 0 at a depth of 0.09. Together they pick a place on the spectrum: warm on left-facing edges, cool on right-facing ones, white inside.

Step 6 of 8

Cut the screen into rows of bars

On a landscape screen there are 24 rows. About every three seconds each row rolls a level ℓ\ellℓ of 0, 1 or 2, which doubles its number of bars each time, and its bars creep sideways at a speed of their own.

r=⌊24 y⌋,Nr=⌊18 a⌋⋅2ℓrr=\lfloor 24\,y\rfloor,\qquad N_r=\lfloor 18\,a\rfloor\cdot2^{\ell_r}r=⌊24y⌋,Nr​=⌊18a⌋⋅2ℓr​

Step 7 of 8

One look-up per bar

A pixel finds the centre c\mathbf{c}c of the bar it sits in and reads the stored shape there, not at its own position. So a whole bar is lit or dark together, in one flat colour, and that is why the outline comes out in steps. φr\varphi_rφr​ is the row's sideways offset.

Step 8 of 8

Pointer and scroll

Rows within about a row and a half of the pointer go one level finer. While the page scrolls, each row's offset φr\varphi_rφr​ is pushed sideways in proportion to the scroll speed, so the bars shear and then settle.

Your turn

Try changing

  • 1.61.61.6 seconds: how long the paint stays.

  • Rows and bars: fewer make the picture chunkier, more make it finer.

  • Bar width: the 0.42 that sets how much of its cell a bar fills.

  • Turn ASCII on. The bars are skipped and the stored shape drives the characters instead.
