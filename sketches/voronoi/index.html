Voronoi Cells

How it works

Voronoi Cells

The screen is divided into a grid of squares with one wandering point in each. Every pixel takes the colour of whichever point is nearest, and that alone carves the plane into cells.

6 steps. Scroll to begin ↓

Step 1 of 6

Lay down a grid

The screen is scaled so that it is five squares tall. The pointer nudges the whole grid by up to a quarter of a square.

u=5 (x RxRy, y)+12(m−12)\mathbf{u}=5\,\big(x\,\tfrac{R_x}{R_y},\ y\big)+\tfrac12\big(\mathbf{m}-\tfrac12\big)u=5(xRy​Rx​​, y)+21​(m−21​)

Step 2 of 6

One seed per square

Square g\mathbf{g}g (a pair of whole numbers) owns one seed. Two random numbers rg\mathbf{r}_{\mathbf{g}}rg​ give its two coordinates a starting phase each, and both swing back and forth at the same rate. That moves the seed round a small ellipse every 12.6 seconds, always inside the middle 80% of its square.

sg=g+12+0.4sin⁡ ⁣(0.5t+2π rg)\mathbf{s}_{\mathbf{g}}=\mathbf{g}+\tfrac12+0.4\sin\!\big(0.5t+2\pi\,\mathbf{r}_{\mathbf{g}}\big)sg​=g+21​+0.4sin(0.5t+2πrg​)

Step 3 of 6

Nearest seed wins

Because seeds stay inside their own squares, the nearest one is almost always in the pixel's own square or one of the eight around it. So only those nine are checked.

d=min⁡g ∥u−sg∥d=\min_{\mathbf{g}}\ \lVert\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{s}_{\mathbf{g}}\rVertd=gmin​ ∥u−sg​∥

Step 4 of 6

A colour for each cell

The winning square has one random number, its id\mathrm{id}id. Three sines of it, at different rates, make red, green and blue. Neighbouring cells get unrelated colours, and each colour cycles slowly.

Step 5 of 6

Dots and shading

A white dot marks each seed, where ddd is under 0.05, and each cell darkens toward its rim because ddd grows away from the seed. The cell walls are never drawn: you see them only where the colour changes from one cell to the next.

Step 6 of 6

A trace of the last frame

A small part of the previous frame is mixed in, which softens the moment a pixel changes cell. The final pass adds 20% saturation and darkens the corners.
